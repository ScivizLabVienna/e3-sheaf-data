# Pre-submission Review — EM-MIR Workshop (v0.1 draft)

## 1. SUMMARY

The authors formalise adjacent-frame displacement measurements as a 1-cochain on a coverage nerve and use triangle-closure residuals (a coboundary defect) as a parameter-free per-edge QC signal for volume-EM registration, pre-registering acceptance gates on three EMPIAR channels. The headline result is negative: no channel meets AUROC ≥ 0.9 at ≤ 3×σ_c, which the authors interpret as evidence that detectability is bounded by channel physics rather than detector design, and that the photogrammetry convention of pruning at 2–3×RMS does not transfer to deformation-dominated volume EM.

## 2. VERDICT: **Borderline** (workshop bar)

For an EM-MIR workshop this clears the novelty bar — nobody in this community has pre-registered a coboundary-residual QC detector on real SBF/FIB-SEM archives and reported honest nulls with git-frozen artifacts. The algebraic framing is clean and the disclosure protocol (§4, instrument E6b) is a genuine methodological contribution to agent-run science that this audience will appreciate. However, the three "headline" claims are stated more broadly than the evidence supports (see §5), there is zero comparison against EMSIQA/DEMIS/learned-feature baselines on the same tiles, and the scope (K = 6, one defect class, two detector families) is too narrow to sustain words like "falsified" and "theorem." A revised version with at least one learned-method comparison and toned-down language would be a solid workshop contribution; as-is it reads like a well-executed internal report that hasn't yet been stress-tested against the community's existing tools.

## 3. TOP-3 STRENGTHS (as reviewers will see them)

1. **Pre-registration discipline with public artifacts.** Every gate is dated before its run, bootstrap CIs are frozen in git, and the self-referential-anchor bug was caught algebraically *before* seeing a gate verdict. In a community where "we tried five thresholds and reported the best AUROC" is still common (EMSIQA's own evaluation protocol has been criticised for this), this is genuinely refreshing and citable as a methodological template.

2. **The gauge-invariance observation, even if trivial, is stated precisely and tested empirically.** The fact that N_shift yields AUROC *exactly* 0.500 (not "approximately") on real phase-correlation data validates the instrument construction in a way no learned metric can. Reviewers familiar with Hodge-based global registration (Carlino et al.) will recognise this as the dual of the loop-elimination argument and appreciate that it is now framed as a *detectability limit* rather than an algorithmic step.

3. **The FIB-SEM inversion result is operationally useful.** Showing that vendor TTrAC pre-registration collapses σ_c by ~15× yet does *not* make small defects detectable (AUROC still < 0.7 at 5×σ_c) directly challenges the implicit assumption in EMSIQA/DEMIS pipelines that lower residual noise ⇒ better QC sensitivity. This is a concrete, actionable warning for anyone calibrating learned QA metrics on pre-registered stacks.

## 4. TOP-3 WEAKEST POINTS (with the reviewer's exact question)

1. **No comparison to any existing method.** The paper positions itself against EMSIQA, DEMIS, JSEE features, and SAVIOR in §1 but never runs a single one of them on the same tiles or injection trials.
   > *Reviewer question:* "You claim your coboundary detector is 'channel-physics-bound' and that learned detectors add nothing (GLR vs max-abs differ by < 0.03). But you never ran EMSIQA's two-stage error detector or DEMIS's OFSIQA score on these same three channels at the same injection levels. How do you know a learned pairwise metric wouldn't cross AUROC 0.9 at ≤ 2×σ_c where your algebraic statistic fails? Without this, 'channel-physics-bound' is an untested conjecture about *all* statistics, not just the two you tried."

2. **The "falsification" of 2–3×RMS pruning is scoped to one defect type and one window size.** M_edge at K = 6 with a single injected offset is the simplest possible corruption. Real SBF/FIB-SEM defects are spatially extended (warping, charging streaks, section folds), and photogrammetry pruning operates on *all* residuals simultaneously in a global least-squares solve, not per-triangle.
   > *Reviewer question:* "You test exactly one defect class (a single-edge offset) at K = 6 and conclude that '2–3×RMS pruning is falsified on all three channels.' Wang et al. 2014 prune in a global bundle adjustment with hundreds of observations per node. Did you test spatially extended defects, multiple simultaneous corruptions, or the actual global-solve regime? If not, what you have shown is that *per-triangle* closure residuals at K = 6 are insensitive to single-edge offsets at 2–3×σ_c — which is a much narrower claim than 'falsified.'"

3. **Single FIB-SEM stack with no independent ground truth.** The authors acknowledge this in §5 but the entire "channel-physics-bound" narrative for FIB-SEM rests on one vendor-registered MITOMAP tile where shifts are baked by NEWSTACK and verified only by header bytes.
   > *Reviewer question:* "Your FIB-SEM result is the linchpin of the 'closure-σ self-calibration does not normalise detection across channels' claim, yet it is a single stack with no independent registration ground truth (you state shifts are baked by NEWSTACK). How do you know σ_c = 0.11–0.14 px reflects true channel noise rather than an artifact of TTrAC's smoothing? A second FIB-SEM stack registered independently, or even a synthetic deformation field applied to the same tile with known ground truth, would be needed before generalising from n = 1."
